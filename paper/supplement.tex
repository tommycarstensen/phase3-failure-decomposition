\section{Subject drugs and programs (\code{subjects.py}, \code{dedup\_arms.py})}\label{supp:subjects}
Intervention lists mix the investigational drug with comparators and background therapy. Each trial's subject drug is the experimental-arm drug that is rarest across the results cohort, after setting aside \num{dedup.backbones} drugs that are an active comparator in at least \num{dedup.backboneMinTrials} trials and at least as often as they are experimental. Of the \num{cohort.results} trials with posted results, \num{dedup.withArms.pct}\% have arm groups recorded and \num{dedup.withExperimental.pct}\% have an arm of type experimental. \num{dedup.withSubject} trials have an identifiable subject drug, \num{dedup.subjects} distinct drugs in all. The most frequent subjects (Table~\ref{tab:subjects}) are experimental-arm drugs and not comparators or chemotherapy backbones, which is what the rule is meant to achieve; the rule was not audited beyond this.

\section{Therapeutic areas (\code{areas.py}, \code{stratify.py}, \code{inference.py})}\label{supp:areas}
Condition terms were mapped to six areas by an ordered keyword match; a trial with terms in two areas goes to the first that matches. The keywords were checked against the condition terms they capture among the trials of the main window, but the assignment of individual trials was not audited, and the areas are coarse. Asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease are counted under Immunology/Inflammation. Other/rare is not only rare disease: it holds what the five named areas do not, among them ophthalmology, pain and anesthesia, hematology, renal disease, women's health and bone disease.

Under the stripped reading the trend test across \trendyears\ gives $p=\num{trend.naive.p}$.
